STUDENT CAREER GUIDE · 8 MIN READ

Engineering internships: build a portfolio that proves your skills

A useful internship application does not need a long list of certificates. It needs clear evidence that you can learn, build, test, explain and improve something.

1. Choose a role, not just a company name

Decide whether you are applying for software engineering, AI/ML, information systems, cybersecurity, product design or research. A focused application helps reviewers understand what you want to contribute and which work samples matter.

2. Build two or three explainable projects

Choose projects you can describe end to end. For software, include a working demo, clear setup instructions and tests. For AI/ML, document dataset sources, evaluation methods, limitations and failure cases. For cybersecurity, use authorised labs and deliberately vulnerable practice environments—never test systems without permission. For space software, a well-documented simulation or public-data analysis can demonstrate engineering thinking without claiming flight hardware experience.

3. Make your repository reviewable

Use a clear README, setup steps, a short architecture note, tests and known limitations. Keep secrets and credentials out of source control. Explain your contribution when a project was built with a team or AI assistance.

4. Write a concise application

Include your course, year, preferred role, availability, one or two relevant project links and what you hope to learn. Avoid generic copy-and-paste messages. A short, specific explanation is more useful than unsupported claims about expertise.

5. Ask what the internship actually includes

Before accepting, ask for written details about duration, weekly expectations, supervisor, project deliverables, evaluation, certificate conditions and whether a stipend applies. Do not assume every internship is paid or that a certificate or placement is guaranteed unless the official offer says so.

6. Check recruitment safety

Use official company channels, verify the sender and never share passwords, OTPs or bank credentials. Be cautious of unexpected fees or promises of guaranteed jobs. A legitimate process should clearly explain its requirements and terms.
