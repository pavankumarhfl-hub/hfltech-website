Superman by HFL Tech

Superman is the project name for HFL Tech’s humanoid robotics direction. Its engineering direction brings together robotics, AI systems, perception, planning, interaction and safety-conscious engineering.

What the robotics programme explores

The programme explores how humanoid robotics may support human work through perception, planning, interaction and controlled physical actions. Its engineering direction prioritises practical usefulness, transparent system behaviour and meaningful human oversight.

How AgentMesh powers the robotics project

AgentMesh provides the AI agent layer that connects context, planning, tools, execution, evaluation, safety, and human approval. The robotics project aims to connect this software layer with physical perception and action; the robotics vision connects intelligent software with physical perception and action through carefully designed interfaces.

What is being developed?

Perception and context. The project explores how a humanoid robot could interpret its surroundings and use relevant context to support a task. This includes understanding what is happening around it and identifying which information matters before taking action.

Planning and decision-making. A useful robotics system needs to break a goal into manageable steps, account for constraints, and adjust when circumstances change. Planning and decision support focus on breaking goals into steps, accounting for constraints and adapting when circumstances change.

Tools and physical action. Agent software may eventually connect to authorised tools and robotics interfaces so that plans can be translated into controlled actions. These interfaces are designed around clear permissions, careful testing and predictable behaviour.

Human-robot interaction. The project considers how people would give instructions, understand what the system is doing, and intervene when necessary. Human oversight is an important design principle, especially when actions affect people or the physical environment.

Safety and approval. Meaningful actions should operate within defined boundaries, with additional confirmation or review where appropriate. The intended approach includes testing failure cases and making it clear when a system should pause or ask for human help.

Evaluation and reporting. Progress is measured through repeatable tests, engineering reviews, failure analysis and technical evidence. HFL Tech will share verified demonstrations and technical updates as the project develops.

Building human-first robotics

Superman brings together a long-term robotics vision and an engineering-first approach. HFL Tech advances the project through thoughtful system design, safety-conscious development, testing and evidence-led milestones. The defining principle remains human-first: technology should serve people, keep important actions understandable and earn trust through engineering.
