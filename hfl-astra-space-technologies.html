THE BIGGER PICTURE

Build understanding before building hardware.

Space systems are not a single invention. They are a chain of disciplines: physics, materials, electronics, software, manufacturing, testing and operations. Astra’s direction is to explore that chain in a deliberate order—beginning with learning, simulation and software, then developing deeper engineering capability as the work grows.

The goal is clear: make complex space technology understandable, and build the foundations needed to work on increasingly ambitious systems over time.

FUTURE PROJECT DIRECTIONS

From mission software to space infrastructure.

These are long-term areas for research and capability-building—not claims that flight hardware, launch vehicles or operational services already exist.

01 / NEAR-TERM LEARNING

Mission simulation & ground systems

Develop software concepts for mission planning, telemetry visualisation, orbit visualisation, digital mission rehearsals and ground-operations workflows.

02 / SATELLITE SYSTEMS

Small satellite platforms

Study CubeSat-class architectures, payload integration, power budgets, communications, onboard computing and Earth-observation applications.

03 / PROPULSION RESEARCH

Propulsion & vehicle systems

Build foundational knowledge through safe simulation and systems studies covering propulsion cycles, tanks, structures, thermal loads and guidance concepts.

04 / FUTURE LAUNCH

Launch vehicles & reusability

Long-term research direction: launch-system architecture, recovery concepts, reusability trade-offs, vehicle health monitoring and mission reliability.

05 / SPACE APPLICATIONS

Earth observation & connectivity

Explore how satellite data, remote sensing, communications and analytics could support agriculture, disaster response, infrastructure and public services.

06 / CAPABILITY BUILDING

Test, validate, collaborate

Work toward partnerships with universities, laboratories, aerospace suppliers, experienced engineers and institutions to validate each stage responsibly.
